01

The problems

What the standard claims but cannot show.

Found by review, by measurement, and by building

Three things the standard says that it cannot currently support

C8 · Tiers · P01

“Nobody left to trust”

Tier 3 says anyone can check the evidence with published tools. Deploying on Intel TDX forced us to enumerate five parties a verifier must still trust, four of them silently and permanently.

Found with a hardware measurement, not an argument. Paper drafted, tool shipped.

C4 / C7 · P02

Bytes are not effects

Theorem 1 claims execution fidelity by digest comparison. Canonical byte equality binds a request representation. It does not bind what an endpoint does with it: defaults, retries, aliases, redirects.

Everything downstream inherits the gap: complete mediation, self-enforcing execution, the Tier 3 and 4 claims.

C4 · Authorization · P04

A null result that tests nothing

The paper reports that sweeping the bounded-summary label bound made no difference. It could not: no policy decision ever reads the labels. The experiment is presented as evidence the bound is harmless.

The cheapest correction in the roadmap, and it fixes a claim currently in print.

What one TDX measurement asks you to believe · P01

The roadmap

What gets built, in order.

Sequence, not schedule · the roadmap states effort, not dates

Five phases, each pairing papers with the tool that answers them

01

Done

P01 · P02 · P05

The corrective work, and the largest open design question

Trust calculus, effect binding, unlinkable identity. Shipped as parallax, transit and occultation, with poc-audit drawing the map of what remains.

02

Do cheaply, soon

P03 · P04 · P08 → T5 · T2

Small effort, existing apparatus, one correction to a claim in print

Attestation freshness feeds parallax’s challenge and staleness bound. Log concurrency is answered by building ephemeris, the recorder that closes thirteen fields.

03

The real research

P06 · P07 → T6 · T3

Delegation without amplification, and evidence that survives a boundary

The delegation chain stays unnamed until P06 says what it must guarantee. transit guard learns to write down the three fields it already computes.

04

Longer horizon

P09 · P10 → T4

Zero-knowledge evidence, and what belongs in an agent bill of materials

spectrum digests a declared component set and expects a negative result. P09 would replace the record rather than fill a row in it.

05

Institutional

P11 · P12 → no tool

Who pays for verification, and why assurance regimes fail

Ranked last because they are not technical, and possibly more determinative of adoption than anything above them.

Both real, both load-bearing

Two conflicts we have not resolved

Both get worse the moment the recorder exists. Neither has an answer in the roadmap, and building converts each from a footnote into a decision somebody has to make.

The chain links everything

chain_head and merkle_root link every action by an agent to every other one, by construction. That is precisely the property occultation exists to measure the loss of, and P05 argues it should not be assumed. Building ephemeris means shipping the thing the programme’s own research argues against.

Fail-open against fail-closed

parallax-proxy and transit guard refuse requests. occultation-gateway never does, and that guarantee was defended across eight tasks of review: a meter must not be able to take down the API it fronts. Composing all three in one request path has no agreed answer.

Written down so it can be argued with rather than absorbed

Four places the prioritization is most likely wrong

01P11 and P12 may be underratedCertificate Transparency succeeded because browsers mandated it, not because the Merkle tree was elegant. If adoption is decided by institutions, the most important paper here is ranked tenth.
02P05 may be overrated per effortA large cryptographic undertaking with a real chance of a negative result. The measurement half is small and decisive, and was done first for that reason.
03P04 may be too small to be a paperRanked fourth on importance, not on publishability. It may be a section of a stronger paper on bounded runtime monitors, or simply a correction.
04The corrective biasThree of the top four fix our own errors. Somebody without our history would rank P05 and P06 higher: a specification’s errors matter more to its authors than to anyone else.

The asks

Where the programme needs other hands.

Five asks, each traceable to a gap the roadmap names

What we need from the room

01Take a paperNine are unwritten, and each file is scoped to be handed straight to a research pass. P03, P04 and P08 are weeks of work on apparatus that already exists.
02Run it on a real systemThe evidence base is five self-authored deployments. Put parallax-attest on a workload, parallax-proxy in front of a client, and run poc-audit on the records you already have.
03Lend hardware beyond one cloudEvery number is Intel TDX on GCP c3-standard-4. P03 needs the quote cost decomposed on more platforms, and the apparatus already deploys, measures and tears down.
04Decide what code cannotLog granularity, the chain against unlinkability, fail-open against fail-closed. These are working-group decisions, and the eight findings are drafted and waiting to be filed upstream.
05Argue with the prioritiesThe order is one person’s assessment, written down to be contested. If you would rank P05 and P06 above the corrections, say so and we will change it.

Three ways to engage with the lab

Pick the level of commitment that fits. Each one moves a paper.

01 · Member

Show up and weigh in

Attend the working-group meetings. Give feedback on papers and tools as they take shape.

The lightest commitment, and the one that keeps the work honest.

02 · Mentor

Bring the problems, guide the people

Provide problems worth working on from your own domain expertise, inside the six domains. Mentor fellows through their papers.

Your experience becomes the research question.

03 · Fellow

Take a paper to the finish line

Carry one research paper from origin to publication as first author, with a mentor and a working group behind you.

Nine papers are open, and each is scoped to be picked up.
