Proof-of-Control
For the people who sign off

From the Open Verification Lab: what an AI agent can prove about itself today, what it cannot yet, and what we are building next.

Moving trust at the speed of machines

v1.0 working draft · September 2026

The question at the centre of the Open Verification Lab

When AI agents grow more capable and act faster than anyone can watch, how will anyone know what they did, and stop them when they go too far?

Who starts the action

Trust has moved twice. It is moving again.

Until the 1990s

Human → Human

A person stood behind every action.

The last thirty years

Human → Machine

Software waits to be told. You can ask what happened.

Now

Machine → Machine

Agents act at machine speed. Nobody is in the loop to ask.

After-the-fact review cannot keep up. Trust has to come from evidence the action leaves behind.

Proof-of-Control standardv1.0 working draft · public comment to 30 Oct 2026

An industry-led open standard for openly verifying that an AI agent stayed within the controls it was given. Co-chairs Ken Huang and Tricia Wang.

ParallaxResidual trust calculus · Tier 3 reference tool

Computes what you still have to trust in hardware attestation, from real attestation quotes, scored by principal, capability, impact and detection latency.

TransitMessage authorization to effect binding

When does a signed capability bound to a request strictly enforce the action an endpoint performs.

OccultationAccountable but unlinkable identity

What verifiable unlinkability costs at agent action rates. Real BBS+ over BLS12-381, an Ed25519 baseline.

Cryptographic BoundariesPaper · led by Hart Montgomery, LF Decentralized Trust

The formal limits of cryptographic verification for machine execution: bounded claims, non-determinism, primitive-agnostic design.

OVALOpen Verification Agent Leash · Tiers 3 and 4

Reference implementation running agent loops inside hardware enclaves: an AI bill of materials at boot, scope-attenuating prompt charters, C2PA provenance.

Transparency logPublic evidence log

A Merkle-tree log where agent event roots are anchored, so rewriting history afterwards gets caught.

ContainmentEvidence-triggered halt · self-enforcing tier

Refuses an agent’s in-scope actions when its evidence fails, and holds it until a person re-grades its keys and claims.

QFire and HAARFRuntime firewalls

Open firewall architectures from protected-health-information and prompt-injection defence; a seed environment for domain-specific tiers.

The landscape

Whose shoulders this work stands on.

Nobody builds this alone

Six things already exist. The receipt is what joins them.

Each solves its own problem well. None produces a signed, checkable record of what an agent did.

LayerWhat it givesWhat it leaves open
Proof of what code ranConfidential computing and remote attestationA chip signs a statement about the software it is runningSays nothing about whether that software obeyed the rules
Tamper-evident logsTransparency logs, as used to police web certificatesRecords that cannot be quietly rewrittenBuilt for certificates and software releases, not agent actions
Who is allowed to do whatModern authorization tokens and agent identity schemesGranting and narrowing authority, proving identityNo evidence afterwards that the grant was honoured
Where to interceptVendor and industry runtime-control specificationsThe eight moments in an agent’s life where a check can happenThe check lives inside the runtime it is checking, and writes no receipt
Watching it runObservability and tracing standardsRich operational telemetryThe system under scrutiny writes its own log
Governance frameworksNIST AI RMF · ISO/IEC 42001 · SOC 2Process, policy and periodic attestationProof about one agent, in one session, that anyone can check

Say their names

The work this builds on, and what each gave

IETF RATS working groupGave the field a formal grammar for hardware attestation and claims tokens. Proof-of-Control uses it to prove the interceptor itself runs in confidential hardware.
Sigstore and TrillianProved that verification scales through append-only transparency logs. Proof-of-Control turns static artifact signing into continuous action logging.
The Biscuit communityPioneered offline cryptographic attenuation with embedded Datalog. Proof-of-Control leans on it so an agent can never delegate more than it was granted.
Microsoft Responsible AI, Agent HooksNamed the lifecycle moments where an agent can be intercepted. Proof-of-Control moves that in-process contract out of process and makes it non-bypassable.
Cloud Security Alliance, AARMDefined the runtime policy framework for approving, modifying or denying agent tool calls. Proof-of-Control is the ledger that proves those decisions were carried out.
Catalini, Hui and WuFormalized why verification becomes the scarce resource as the cost of doing collapses. The economic argument this programme rests on.

The roadmap

What comes next, in order.

Project spotlight · the Lab’s new program

Proof-of-Control says what an agent did. Containment says what happens next.

When an agent’s evidence goes anomalous, its in-scope actions halt, the halt sits outside the operator’s control, and its keys and claims are re-graded by a person. Triggered by evidence facts, never by a judgment about intent.

Open issue 1

Whether conformance is suspended on a detected control failure, and who holds the halt.

Tier 4

Whether capability-bound dispatch, which needs relying-party cooperation, should be mandatory.

Availability

Expected halt conditions, recovery procedure, and the maximum tolerable outage of proof-gated operation.

Sequence, not schedule · effort, not dates

Five phases, from what is done to what decides adoption

Decisions, not engineering

Two things nobody can code around

Both are trade-offs a working group has to settle. Security leaders are the right people in the room.
