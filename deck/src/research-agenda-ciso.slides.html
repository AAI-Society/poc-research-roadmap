Proof-of-Control
For the people who sign off

What an AI agent can prove about itself today, what it cannot yet, and what we are building next.

Moving trust at the speed of machines

September 2026

Trust that can be checked is the only kind that scales.

Every other kind asks you to take someone’s word for it.

Who starts the action

Trust has moved twice. It is moving again.

Until the 1990s

Human → Human

A person stood behind every action.

The last thirty years

Human → Machine

Software waits to be told. You can ask what happened.

Now

Machine → Machine

Agents act at machine speed. Nobody is in the loop to ask.

After-the-fact review cannot keep up. Trust has to come from evidence the action leaves behind.

What Proof-of-Control asks for

Every action an agent takes leaves a receipt

Signed, tamper-evident, and checkable by anyone with the published tools. The vendor’s word is no longer the only evidence you have.

Where we are today

The receipt is defined. Nobody is writing one yet.

We built the auditors before the receipt. Writing it is the next thing we build, and it is engineering, not research.

Asking the hardware for proof takes longer than the action itself

So proofs are taken once and reused. Every action rests on a proof taken earlier.

If the code changed between the proof and the action, nothing in the receipt tells you. How often to re-prove is an open question.

The proof exists. The receipt does not, yet.

That is the gap the roadmap closes, in order.
